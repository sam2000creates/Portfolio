01 / Selected work

From signal to shipped.

A selection of product decisions from a B2C mobile and web product serving more than one million users.

Feature / V3
01
Feature adoption

Fixing a feature nobody wanted to use.

  • Low usage identified through Mixpanel analytics and customer feedback.
  • Confusing UI made it difficult to understand what users spent and what they could earn.
  • Reward experience felt weak because valuable prizes were shown but rarely won.
  • Root causes informed the proposal for Version 3, with clearer communication and a stronger reward experience.
20 → 50%feature adoption
V3product direction
League
02
Engagement & growth

Turning drop-off into social competition.

  • User drop-off identified as an opportunity to create a stronger reason to return and compete.
  • Research inputs: surveys, customer feedback and existing behavioural data.
  • Points-based Leadership League with player requests to join and up to 50 players per league.
  • Wireframes created from scratch in Figma; cross-functional delivery across product, design, development and QA.
+3%average ARPU QoQ
50players max / league
Onboarding
03
Retention

Improving the first experience.

  • Day 7 retention baseline: 10%.
  • Onboarding redesigned to be easier to understand and more visually engaging; tutorial steps modernised.
  • Tutorial and event UI updated.
  • A/B tests used to compare onboarding gameplay mechanics; higher-retention variant selected for rollout.
10 → 12%Day 7 retention
A/Btested decisions
Delivery
04
Delivery transformation

Making delivery more flexible.

  • Four-week sprints limited flexibility for bugs, user needs and smaller product opportunities.
  • Shifted delivery to two-week cycles for more frequent releases.
  • Created UAT guidelines where none existed before, and standardised rollout workflows from development through release.
  • Focus: faster bug fixes, smaller releases and earlier delivery of useful changes.
75 → 90%on-time release rate
2×feature & bug throughput

03 / Decision making

Different decisions
need different tools.

I do not force every product question into one framework. I choose the lightest method that fits the decision.

Roadmap & backlog prioritisation

Choose what deserves roadmap space.

  • RICE — for roadmap initiatives, considering Reach, Impact, Confidence and Effort.
  • MoSCoW — for backlog prioritisation and clarifying Must, Should, Could and Won't priorities.
  • Capacity, dependencies, constraints and customer/business value remain part of the final decision.
RICEMoSCoW
Quick team calls

Keep smaller decisions simple.

  • Value vs Effort — for quick team decisions where a lightweight trade-off is enough.
  • The framework supports the conversation; it does not replace product judgement.
